# IELTS Speaking Part 2: techniques for story mapping

This reference governs answer preparation and practice, not topic-bank selection. Continue using the user-supplied 2026 snapshot or 同桌英语 according to `SKILL.md`.

## Task and scoring

Part 2 gives the learner one minute to prepare and asks for a one-to-two-minute talk. The cue card names a subject, supporting points, and an aspect to explain. The examiner may ask one or two follow-up questions and stops the talk when time is up. The talk should stay on the card's actual subject and develop ideas logically. [IELTS test format](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-speaking)

Speaking is assessed across four equally weighted criteria: fluency and coherence, lexical resource, grammatical range and accuracy, and pronunciation. Do not promise a score from a template, a word count, or a set number of stories. [IELTS scoring](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/your-results/ielts-scoring-in-detail), [band descriptors](https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-speaking-band-descriptors.pdf)

## Apply this to the story bank

1. **Choose the subject first.** Identify whether the card asks for a person, place, object, event, activity, opinion change, future plan, etc., and check constraints such as childhood, recent, often, or successful. Select a story only when it can genuinely answer that subject. A shared word alone is insufficient. [IELTS test format](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-speaking), [Cambridge IELTS FAQ](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/it/Images/269898-ielts-academic-faqs.pdf)
2. **Prepare cues, not a script.** In one minute, jot short phrases in speaking order for the card's points. English phrases can save translation time, but the operational goal is notes the learner can use instantly. Start with the first specific example they can describe easily; a real or well-known experience generally offers more usable detail. [IELTS preparation-time guidance](https://ielts.org/news-and-insights/how-to-use-the-preparation-time-in-ielts-speaking-part-2)
3. **Develop one concrete scene.** After directly naming the subject, add when/where/who, what happened, one observable action or detail, and why it mattered or how the learner felt. Cover the card's points naturally, giving more space to its final explanatory prompt. These are content anchors, not mandatory sentence slots. [British Council speaking preparation](https://takeielts.britishcouncil.org/prepare/ielts-academic/speaking), [IDP cue-card guidance](https://ielts.idp.com/thailand/about/news-and-articles/article-cue-card-topics-for-speaking-test)
4. **Extend only in relevant directions.** If the talk ends early, explain cause, consequence, contrast, a small anecdote, or a specific feeling. Past–present–future can help when it naturally fits; it is an optional extension, not a required formula. Do not drift away from the named subject merely to fill time. [British Council common mistakes](https://takeielts.britishcouncil.org/blog/common-mistakes-ielts), [British Council PPF lesson](https://takeielts.britishcouncil.org/sites/default/files/speaking_part_2_-_ppf_strategy.pdf)
5. **Rehearse under exam timing.** Practise with one minute of notes and up to two minutes of speaking, record a response, then review whether it answered the card, stayed coherent, avoided excessive hesitation or repetition, used accurate language, and remained easy to understand. Adjust the cue notes and story details, then try a different card with the same story. [IELTS practice activity](https://ielts.org/news-and-insights/using-impromptu-speaking-tasks-to-prepare-learners-for-ielts-speaking), [IDP practice advice](https://ielts.idp.com/bangladesh/prepare/speaking/part-2-topics-and-questions)

## Optional IMF outline (user-provided)

IMF means **Introduction → Main points → Feelings or Future**. It is a practical speaking outline supplied by the learner, not an official IELTS scoring formula. Use it to organize a story after confirming that the story fits the cue card.

| Segment | Two-minute practice target | What to say |
|---|---:|---|
| Introduction | About 15 seconds | Name the exact person, place, object, or experience requested; give one identifying detail. |
| Main points | About 90 seconds | Cover the card's prompts with a concrete scene, actions, relevant details, and an explanation. |
| Feelings or Future | About 15 seconds | Explain the significance, feeling, or future consequence that actually fits the card. |

The learner's original 20 + 90 + 20 seconds totals **130 seconds**, so these are adjusted practice targets, not rigid quotas. If the final cue asks *why*, *whether it was difficult*, or *why it is popular*, answer that specific question; do not substitute a generic feeling or future sentence. The official limit is one to two minutes. [IELTS Part 2 format](https://ielts.org/take-a-test/test-types/ielts-academic-test/ielts-academic-format-speaking)

For the opening, a natural option is “I'd like to talk about ...” or “The first person/place that comes to mind is ...”. Attach the actual subject immediately: “I'd like to talk about my grandfather, whose birthday we celebrated last Labor Day.” “I'm going to tell you about ...” is another option. Vary wording when it feels natural; do not force the same stock opener into every answer. The body and ending must differ by cue card even when the underlying story is reused. The learner also supplied examples about jeans, a laptop, and a swimming pool; write “a piece of electronic equipment” or “my laptop”, not “one of my electronic equipment”.

## Guardrail for 串题

Three or four stories are a **retrieval framework**. Keep stable facts, but change the answer's subject, selected details, and explanation for each card. Do not supply the same memorized speech with a different opening sentence. Cambridge warns that memorized answers may miss the question, and British Council advises natural speech rather than rehearsed responses. [Cambridge IELTS FAQ](https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/it/Images/269898-ielts-academic-faqs.pdf), [British Council speaking preparation](https://takeielts.britishcouncil.org/prepare/ielts-academic/speaking)
